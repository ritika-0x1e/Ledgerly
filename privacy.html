Ledgerly privacy policy

Last updated 24 November 2026

Ledgerly is a paper-style monthly expense notebook that runs in your phone's browser. In short: your notes stay on your phone, and the only copy that ever leaves it is the Google Drive backup you choose to turn on, which goes into your own Drive.

What stays on your device

Everything you write (months, expense lines, tags, settings) is stored only in your browser on your device. There is no Ledgerly account and no Ledgerly server that receives your notes. Nothing is tracked, there are no ads, and no analytics.

Google Drive backup (optional)

  • If you tap Connect Google Drive, you sign in with a Google account of your choice and allow Ledgerly the drive.file permission. With it, Ledgerly can see and change only the files it creates itself, never anything else in your Drive.
  • Ledgerly creates a folder called Ledgerly in that Drive and saves one text file per month in it. These files go straight from your phone to Google; they do not pass through any other server.
  • To keep backups working without asking you to sign in again, a small sign-in service (a Cloudflare Worker) stores the Google sign-in token for your device. It holds only that token, linked to a random key kept on your phone. It never receives your notes and keeps no logs of them.
  • Ledgerly's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The data is used only to back up your own notes to your own Drive; it is not sold, shared, or used for anything else.

Removing access and data

  • Disconnect Drive in the app's menu revokes the Google permission and deletes the stored sign-in token. You can also remove Ledgerly at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Backup files stay in your Drive until you delete them; they are yours.
  • Clearing the site's data or uninstalling the app removes the notes from your device.
